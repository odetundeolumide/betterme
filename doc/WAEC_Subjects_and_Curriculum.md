# WAEC (WASSCE) Subjects by Department and Curriculum Topics

Compiled 29 September 2026 for Odetunde Olumide.

## Sources and how to read this file

- **Subject lists by department:** WAEC Nigeria official site (waecnigeria.org, subjects page and FAQ).
- **Curriculum topics:** WAEC syllabus text as reproduced on studentship.com.ng (and, for most other subjects, the WASSCE syllabus PDFs hosted at rachel.core2learn.org) (each subject page is linked in its section). These are secondary copies of the official syllabus, mostly dated 2024/2025. WAEC revises syllabi periodically, so confirm against the syllabus your school holds or waecnigeria.org before using this for anything official.
- **Status tag on each subject:** `DONE` means topics were extracted from the source named in that section. Sections say where the source is thin or dated.
- Where a syllabus has Section A (all countries), Section B (Ghana) and Section C (Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Gambia, Liberia), Nigerian candidates cover Sections A and C.

## Key rules to note

- Candidates sit 8 to 9 subjects.
- **Core for 2026 and 2027:** English Language and General Mathematics. Citizenship and Heritage Studies and Digital Technologies become core subjects but will not be examined until 2028.
- Economics and Marketing are listed under all three departments in this file, and Government is listed under both Humanities and Business, as requested. A press report says the 2026 guidelines limit Economics to Business students; WAEC has not confirmed this on its own pages, so check before advising a Science or Humanities student to register for it.

## General Mathematics (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-mathematics-syllabus/
The main topic headings come from studentship.com.ng. The detailed topics and limits below come from the full WAEC syllabus text (2021/2022 edition, the latest full text I could open) reproduced at https://www.ecolebooks.com/waec-syllabus-for-general-mathematics-mathematics-core-2021-2022-wassce/. That is a secondary copy, so check it against the syllabus your school holds. Where I condensed the notes column, the limits are stated in brief.

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes.
- Paper 2: 13 essay questions, answer 10, 100 marks, 2 hours 30 minutes. Section A has five compulsory elementary questions (40 marks). Section B has eight longer questions, answer five (60 marks).
- Non-programmable, silent, cordless calculators are allowed. Phones are not. Mathematical tables may be used, and some questions may prohibit tables and calculators.

**Topics, contents and limits**

Items marked (*) are tested in Section B of Paper 2 only. Some items in the source carry additional country markers that did not survive copying, so confirm those against your school's syllabus.

**A. Number and numeration**
1. **Number bases:** convert from any base to base 10 and back, and between two bases; addition, subtraction and multiplication in other bases.
2. **Modular arithmetic:** the idea of modulo; addition, subtraction and multiplication modulo n; everyday uses (market days, clocks, shift duty).
3. **Fractions, decimals and approximations:** the four operations; approximation and significant figures, kept realistic.
4. **Indices:** laws of indices, including simple negative and fractional indices; standard form (scientific notation).
5. **Logarithms:** link between indices and logarithms; rules of logarithms; use of tables for multiplication, division, powers and roots.
6. **Sequences and series:** patterns of sequences; nth term; arithmetic progression (with its sum) and geometric progression (nth term only, no sum).
7. **Sets:** universal, finite, infinite, empty, disjoint sets and subsets; union, intersection and complement; Venn diagrams of at most three sets.
8. **Logical reasoning:** simple statements, true and false statements, negation, implication.
9. **Integers and rational numbers:** the four operations; number line; notation N, Z and Q.
10. **Surds:** simplify and rationalise simple surds; basic operations.
11. **Matrices and determinants (*):** order and types of matrices (not larger than 3 x 3); addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication and multiplication; determinant of a 2 x 2 matrix; solving two simultaneous equations.
12. **Ratio, proportions and rates:** ratio and proportion; partnerships, rates of work, cost, taxes (VAT, withholding tax), foreign exchange, density, speed.
13. **Percentages:** simple interest, commission, discount, depreciation, profit and loss, compound interest (up to 3 years), hire purchase, percentage error.
14. **Financial arithmetic (*):** depreciation and amortisation; annuities; capital market instruments (shares, stocks, debentures, bonds).
15. **Variation:** direct, inverse, partial and joint variation, with practical problems.

**B. Algebraic processes**
1. **Algebraic expressions:** forming expressions from situations; evaluating them.
2. **Simple operations:** expansion; factorisation (common factor, grouping, difference of two squares, ax2 + bx + c); binary operations on real numbers.
3. **Linear equations:** one variable; two simultaneous equations by elimination, substitution and graphs; word problems.
4. **Change of subject of a formula:** rearranging and substituting.
5. **Quadratic equations:** solving by factorisation, completing the square and the formula; forming an equation from given roots; practical problems.
6. **Graphs of linear and quadratic functions:** reading and drawing graphs; maximum and minimum points; intercepts and axis of symmetry; solving related equations from a graph; graphical solution of a pair of equations; tangents to determine gradient (*).
7. **Linear inequalities:** one variable on the number line; two variables and simultaneous inequalities graphically (*), including simple linear programming.
8. **Algebraic fractions:** operations with monomial and binomial denominators; values for which a fraction is undefined.
9. **Functions and relations (*):** one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-one and many-to-many; mappings and finding the rule.

**C. Mensuration**
1. **Lengths and perimeters:** Pythagoras theorem, sine and cosine rules (*); arcs, sectors and segments; longitudes and latitudes (*).
2. **Areas:** triangles and special quadrilaterals; circles, sectors and segments; surface areas of cubes, cuboids, cylinders, pyramids, prisms, cones and spheres; areas of similar figures and compound shapes.
3. **Volumes:** cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, pyramids, spheres; similar solids; compound shapes.

**D. Plane geometry**
1. **Angles:** angles at a point, on a straight line, and vertically opposite angles.
2. **Parallel lines:** alternate, corresponding and interior angles; intercept theorem (*).
3. **Triangles and polygons:** angle sums, exterior angle, congruence conditions, special triangles and quadrilaterals, similar triangles, angle sums and exterior angles of polygons, parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels.
4. **Circles:** chords; angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angle in a semicircle; angles in the same segment and in opposite segments; tangent perpendicular to radius; alternate segment theorem.
5. **Construction (*):** bisectors; parallel and perpendicular lines; angles such as 90, 60, 45, 30 and combinations; triangles and quadrilaterals from given data.
6. **Loci (*):** points at a given distance from a point or a line; points equidistant from two points or two lines; intersections in two dimensions.

**E. Coordinate geometry of straight lines:** the x-y plane; midpoint and distance between two points; gradient; equation of a line in the forms y = mx + c and y - y1 = m(x - x1).

**F. Trigonometry**
1. **Sine, cosine and tangent:** acute angles and use of tables; ratios of 30, 45 and 60 degrees without tables; angles from 0 to 360 degrees and the unit circle; graphs of sine and cosine.
2. **Angles of elevation and depression:** heights and distances (simple problems).
3. **Bearings (*):** bearing of one point from another; distances and angles; sine and cosine rules may be used.

**G. Introductory calculus (*):** differentiation of algebraic functions (gradient of a curve, standard derivatives, maximum and minimum values, rates of change); integration of simple algebraic functions and evaluation of simple definite integrals.

**H. Statistics and probability**
1. **Statistics:** frequency distributions; pie charts, bar charts, histograms and frequency polygons; mean, median and mode for discrete and grouped data; cumulative frequency curve (ogive); range, inter-quartile range, variance, mean deviation and standard deviation (standard deviation of grouped data is Section B only).
2. **Probability:** experimental and theoretical probability; addition for mutually exclusive events; multiplication for independent events; with replacement, and without replacement (*).

**I. Vectors and transformation (*)**
1. **Vectors in a plane:** directed line segments; Cartesian components; magnitude; equal, zero and parallel vectors; addition, subtraction and multiplication by a scalar.
2. **Transformation in the Cartesian plane:** reflection (mirror lines such as x = k, y = x, y = kx), rotation about the origin and other points (angles from -180 to 180 degrees), translation by a vector, and enlargement with a given centre and scale factor.

**Units to know:** 1000 mm = 100 cm = 1 m; 1000 m = 1 km; 10,000 m2 = 1 hectare; 1000 cm3 = 1 litre; 1000 mg = 1 g; 1000 g = 1 kg; 1000 kg = 1 tonne; 100 kobo = 1 naira.

**Recommended textbooks (per syllabus):** Channon and Smith, *New General Mathematics for West Africa SSS 1 to 3* (Longman); David-Osuagwu et al., *New School Mathematics for Senior Secondary Schools*; Adelodun, *Distinction in Mathematics*; Anyebe, *Basic Mathematics for Senior Secondary Schools*; Ibude et al., *Algebra and Calculus for Schools and Colleges*.

## English Language (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-english-syllabus/

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 80 multiple-choice questions (40 lexis, 40 structure), 40 marks, 1 hour.
- Paper 2: essay, comprehension and summary, 100 marks, 2 hours. Section A is essay writing (answer one of five, 50 marks, minimum 450 words). Section B is comprehension on a passage of at least 350 words (20 marks). Section C is summary on a passage of about 500 words (30 marks).
- Paper 3: Test of Orals for Nigeria and Liberia, 60 multiple-choice questions, 30 marks, 45 minutes.

**Abilities tested:** correct English usage, writing for specified audiences, coherent paragraphing, sentence control, grammar, spelling and punctuation, comprehension of written and spoken English, implied meaning and tone, pronunciation, and summarising.

**Topics**

1. **Paper 1: Lexis**
   - Everyday vocabulary (home, social relationships, core school subjects).
   - General vocabulary in fields such as building and construction, agriculture, fishing, stock exchange, health, environment, culture and ceremonies, law and order, motor vehicles and travel, government and administration, sports, religion, science and technology, animal husbandry, advertising, and the human body systems.
   - Idioms and collocations; figurative usage; synonyms, antonyms, homonyms.
2. **Paper 1: Structure**
   - Word-form changes for number, tense and degree.
   - How word classes combine into groups and sentences.
   - Structural words: conjunctions, determiners, prepositions; sequence of tenses; pronoun-antecedent agreement.
3. **Paper 2 Section A: Essay writing**
   - Letter, speech, narration, description, argument or debate, report, article, exposition, creative writing.
   - Marked on content, organisation, expression and mechanical accuracy.
4. **Paper 2 Section B: Comprehension**
   - Word and phrase equivalents; factual content; inference; expressions of sentiment, emotion and attitude.
   - Identifying and labelling grammatical structures and their functions; basic literary terms; recasting sentences.
5. **Paper 2 Section C: Summary**
   - Extracting relevant points, concise summary without repetition, summarising specific aspects of a passage.
6. **Paper 3: Oral English**
   - Vowels (pure vowels and diphthongs); consonants and consonant clusters; rhymes.
   - Word stress and syllable structure; sentence stress; emphatic stress.
   - Intonation (falling and rising patterns); phonetic symbols.

**Recommended textbooks (per syllabus, selection):** Bamgbose, *English Lexis and Structure for Senior Secondary Schools and Colleges*; Banjo et al., *New Oxford Secondary English Course*; Caesar, *Essential Oral English for Schools and Colleges*; Elugbe, *Oral English for Schools and Colleges*; Egbe, *Mastering English Usage and Communication Skills*; Daniel Jones, *Cambridge English Pronouncing Dictionary*; Ogunsanwo et al., *Countdown English Language*; Tomori, *Objective Tests for School Certificate English*.

## Civic Education (DONE)

Source: WASSCE Civic Education syllabus PDF (rachel.core2learn.org/modules/en-wassce/WASSCESYLABUS/CIVIC_EDUCATION.pdf). Citizenship and Heritage Studies is due to replace it as a core subject from 2028; that syllabus is not covered here.

**Aims:** understand the importance of citizenship; explain the structure, functions and responsibilities of government; know emerging issues in society; understand citizens' rights, duties and obligations; appreciate and uphold democratic values.

**Exam format** (two papers, composite, one sitting)
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions from the whole syllabus, 40 marks, 1 hour.
- Paper 2: essay paper, 60 marks, 2 hours. Sections A, B and C with three questions each; answer four questions, at least one from each section.

**Topics**

*Section A: National ethics, discipline, rights and obligations*
1. Values: definition, types, importance to society.
2. Citizenship and nationalism: meaning of citizenship and citizenship education, goals, duties and obligations of citizens; nationalism and ways of promoting national consciousness, integrity and unity; local and world civic problems.
3. Human rights: meaning, categories, characteristics; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (background, the seven core freedoms, importance, roles of individuals, groups and government); limitations on rights.
4. Law and order: definition, features, importance; constituted authority (meaning, types, roles).
5. Responsible parenthood: meaning, roles, importance in national development.
6. Traffic regulations: meaning, importance, roles of individuals and government.
7. Interpersonal relationships: meaning, types, skills; inter-communal relationships and conflicts, and skills for resolving them.

*Section B: Emerging issues in society*
1. Cultism: meaning, origin, cult groups in Nigeria, reasons, consequences, prevention.
2. Drugs and drug abuse: drugs commonly abused, how, symptoms, behaviour of addicts, prevention, government agencies and laws.
3. Human trafficking: meaning, causes, effects, efforts to stop it.
4. HIV/AIDS: meaning, causes, symptoms and effects, prevention, stigmatisation.
5. Youth empowerment: meaning, skills, benefits, government efforts.

*Section C: Governmental system and processes*
1. Structure and functions of government: meaning, tiers, functions.
2. Democracy, rule of law and national development: meaning, types, features, importance, pillars and problems of democracy; meaning, features, importance and problems of the rule of law; national development and how democracy and the rule of law promote it (good governance, employment creation, poverty alleviation).
3. Political apathy: meaning, causes, consequences, why leaders fail to protect followers' interests and how they can, ways of discouraging apathy.
4. Civil society and popular participation: types and modes (traditional and modern) of participation; civil society (functions, characteristics such as accountability and fairness, problems such as corruption and inadequate funding).
5. Public service in democracy: meaning, functions, problems, reasons for shortcomings, ways of improvement.

**Suggested reading (per syllabus):** the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (as amended); *Civic Education for Senior Secondary, Books 1 to 3* (Learn Africa); *Straight-Gate Civic Education for Senior Secondary Schools*; Anifowose and Enemuo (eds), *Elements of Politics*; among others.

## Biology (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-biology-syllabus/

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 50 minutes.
- Paper 2: essay and short structured questions, 70 marks, 1 hour 40 minutes. Nigerian candidates answer two Section A essays plus the compulsory short structured questions from Section C (ecology).
- Paper 3: practical, 80 marks, 2 hours.

**Topics**

1. **Concept of living**
   - Living and non-living things; classification into five kingdoms (Monera, Protoctista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia); plants versus animals.
   - Organisation of life: cell, tissue, organ, system; forms in which living cells exist (single, colony, filament, part of an organism).
2. **The cell**
   - Cell structure and function of components; plant versus animal cells.
   - The cell and its environment: diffusion, osmosis, active transport.
   - Nutrition: autotrophic (photosynthesis) and heterotrophic (holozoic).
3. **Cellular respiration**: aerobic, anaerobic, energy release.
4. **Excretion (basic)**: excretion in single-celled organisms, waste products of metabolism.
5. **Growth**: cell division (mitosis), enlargement, differentiation; growth hormones and auxins; tropisms; movement (cilia, flagella, cyclosis); asexual and sexual reproduction.
6. **Supporting systems**
   - Animals: skeletal materials (bone, cartilage, chitin), types of skeleton, vertebral column, girdles, limb bones, functions.
   - Plants: supporting tissues and their functions.
7. **Transport system**: need for transport; heart, arteries, veins, capillaries; blood and lymph; water and mineral transport in plants.
8. **Respiratory system**: body surfaces (skin, gills, lungs); gaseous exchange in fish, toads, mammals, plants.
9. **Excretory systems**: kidney, stomata, lenticels; dissection of a small mammal; plant excretory products.
10. **Homeostasis**: kidney, liver, skin.
11. **Hormonal coordination**: animal hormones (site, function, over- and under-secretion); plant hormones.
12. **Nervous coordination**: central and peripheral nervous systems; parts of the brain; spinal cord; neurones; reflex arc; reflex versus voluntary action; conditioned reflex.
13. **Sense organs**: structure and function of the eye and ear.
14. **Reproduction**
    - Mammalian reproductive systems, gametes, fertilisation, embryo development, birth, birth control.
    - Insect metamorphosis (butterfly, cockroach); comparison across fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals.
    - Flowering plants: floral parts, pollination types and agents, fertilisation, fruit types and structure, seed and fruit dispersal.
15. **Plant and animal nutrition**
    - Photosynthesis (process, equation, light and dark reactions, conditions, evidence); mineral requirements.
    - Food classes and sources; balanced diet; digestive enzymes; modes of nutrition (autotrophic, holozoic, parasitic, symbiotic, saprophytic); alimentary systems; dental formula.
16. **Basic ecological concepts**
    - Ecosystem components; ecological factors (physical, edaphic); food chains and webs; trophic levels; energy flow; pyramids; decomposition.
    - Biological associations (parasitism, symbiosis, commensalism, saprophytism); adaptation to habitats.
    - Air, water and soil pollution; ecological succession; factors affecting population size.
    - Food preservation; selected insects and pest control (weevils, cotton strainers).
    - Microorganisms and health: beneficial and harmful effects, diseases (cholera, measles, malaria, ringworm), control methods, public health (refuse and sewage disposal, immunisation).
17. **Conservation of natural resources**: soil, water, wildlife, forest, minerals.
18. **Variation in population**: morphological variation (size, colour, fingerprints); physiological variation (tongue rolling, PTC tasting, ABO blood groups).
19. **Heredity (genetics)**: genetic terms; Mendel's experiments and laws; chromosomes; probability; linkage, sex determination, sex-linked traits; applications in agriculture and medicine.
20. **Adaptation for survival and evolution**: social insects (termites, bees); evidence and theories of evolution.

**Recommended textbooks (per syllabus):** Ndu et al., *Senior Secondary School Biology* (Longman); Odunfa, *Essentials of Biology*; Ogunniyi et al., *Biology for Senior Secondary Schools* (Macmillan); Ramalingam, *Modern Biology*; Stone and Cozens, *Biology for West African Schools*; Usua, *Handbook of Practical Biology*.

## Chemistry (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-chemistry-syllabus/

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 1 hour.
- Paper 2: essay, 100 marks, 2 hours. Section A has ten short structured questions (25 marks). Section B has four questions, answer any three (25 marks each).
- Paper 3: practical, 50 marks, 2 hours (one quantitative analysis question, one qualitative analysis question, one on practical activities in the teaching syllabus).

**Assumed background:** Integrated or Basic Science and Mathematics at JSS level. Candidates must know significant figures, SI units and IUPAC nomenclature.

**Topics**

1. **Introduction to chemistry**: measurement of physical quantities; importance of scientific measurement; scientific method.
2. **Structure of the atom**: gross features; atomic number, mass number, isotopes; relative atomic and molecular mass (carbon-12 scale); nature of matter; physical and chemical changes; electron configuration, orbitals, filling rules.
3. **Separation techniques**: classification of mixtures; separation methods; criteria for purity.
4. **Periodic chemistry**: periodic law and trends; period 3 (Na to Ar); halogens (F, Cl, Br, I); first transition series (Sc to Zn); reactions of metals with acids, and of metal oxides and trioxocarbonates(IV).
5. **Chemical bonds**: ionic, covalent, coordinate (dative), metallic bonding; properties and naming; shapes of molecules; hydrogen bonding, van der Waals forces; comparison of bond types.
6. **Stoichiometry and chemical reactions**: symbols, formulae, equations; empirical and molecular formulae; laws of chemical combination; the mole and mole ratios; solutions, concentration terms, standard solutions, dilution.
7. **States of matter**: kinetic theory; changes of state; diffusion; gases (gas laws, laboratory preparation and properties); liquids and vapour; solids (types, structure, properties, diamond and graphite, melting point determination).
8. **Energy and energy changes**: energy and enthalpy; definitions and illustrations of energy changes.
9. **Acids, bases and salts**: definitions and properties; electrolytes; classification; pH; salt preparation, uses, hydrolysis; deliquescent, efflorescent, hygroscopic compounds; indicators; acid-base titration.
10. **Solubility**: general principles and practical applications.
11. **Chemical kinetics and equilibrium**: rate of reaction, factors, theories, graphs; equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle.
12. **Redox reactions**: oxidation and reduction; oxidising and reducing agents; redox equations; electrochemical cells (electrode potential, cell notation, e.m.f., applications); electrolysis (principles, discharge factors, Faraday's laws, applications); corrosion.
13. **Chemistry of carbon compounds**: classification and functional groups; purification; petroleum; empirical and molecular formulae; homologous series, isomerism; alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, benzene, alkanols, alkanoic acids, alkanoates (sources, properties, tests, uses).
14. **Chemistry, industry and environment**: chemical industry; air, water, soil pollution; biotechnology.
15. **Basic biochemistry and synthetic polymers**: proteins and amino acids; fats and oils (including soap preparation); carbohydrates; synthetic polymers.

**Practical skills:** measuring mass and volume; standard solutions and dilution; filtration, recrystallisation, melting point; heats of neutralisation and solution; pH by colorimetry; rate of reaction from concentration-time curves; equilibrium constants.
**Quantitative analysis:** acid-base titrations (methyl orange, methyl red, phenolphthalein); redox titrations (MnO4- with Fe2+, MnO4- with C2O4 2-, I2 in KI with S2O3 2-).
**Qualitative analysis:** tests for cations (NH4+, Ca2+, Pb2+, Cu2+, Fe2+, Fe3+, Al3+, Zn2+) and anions (Cl-, SO3 2-, CO3 2-, NO3-, SO4 2-); halogen displacement; gas tests (H2, NH3, CO2, HCl, SO2); tests for alkenes, alkanols, alkanoic acids, sugars, starch, proteins.

**Recommended textbooks (per syllabus):** Ababio, *New School Chemistry for Senior Secondary Schools*; Bajah et al., *Senior Secondary Chemistry* (Longman); Ojokuku, *Understanding Chemistry for Schools and Colleges*; Odesina, *Essential Chemistry for Senior Secondary Schools*; Uche et al., *Countdown to WASSCE/SSCE, NECO, JME Chemistry*.


## Physics (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-physics-syllabus/

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 1 hour 15 minutes.
- Paper 2: 60 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes. Section A has seven short structured questions (answer five, 15 marks). Section B has five essay questions (answer three, 45 marks).
- Paper 3: practical, 50 marks, 2 hours 45 minutes (answer two of three questions).

Questions are set in SI units. The syllabus is organised into five conceptual parts.

**Part 1: Interaction of matter, space and time**
1. Concepts of matter; fundamental and derived quantities and units.
2. Position, distance, displacement; mass and weight; time and its measurement.
3. Fluids at rest: volume, density, relative density, pressure in fluids, Archimedes' principle, law of flotation.
4. Motion: types of motion, relative motion, types of force (contact and field), solid friction, viscosity, simple circular motion.
5. Speed and velocity; distance-time and displacement-time graphs.
6. Rectilinear acceleration: velocity-time graphs, equations of motion, motion under gravity.
7. Scalars and vectors: representation, addition, resolution, resultant velocity.
8. Equilibrium of forces: principle of moments, conditions for equilibrium, centre of gravity and stability.
9. Simple harmonic motion: definition, speed and acceleration, period, frequency, amplitude, energy, forced vibration and resonance.
10. Newton's laws of motion: inertia, force and momentum and impulse, action and reaction.

**Part 2: Energy (mechanical and heat)**
1. Forms of energy, world energy resources, conservation of energy.
2. Work, energy and power; potential and kinetic energy; simple machines (levers, pulleys, inclined plane, wedge, screw, wheel and axle, gears).
3. Heat: temperature measurement; effects of heat; linear, area and volume expansivity; conduction, convection, radiation.
4. Gas laws: Boyle's, Charles', pressure law, general gas law.
5. Heat capacity and specific heat capacity; latent heat (fusion and vaporisation); evaporation and boiling; vapour pressure; humidity, relative humidity, dew point.

**Part 3: Waves**
1. Production and propagation of mechanical waves; relationship between frequency, wavelength, period and velocity.
2. Transverse and longitudinal waves; wave properties (reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference, standing waves).
3. Light: sources, rectilinear propagation, reflection (plane, concave, convex mirrors), refraction (blocks, prisms, converging and diverging lenses), optical instruments, dispersion of white light.
4. Electromagnetic spectrum.
5. Sound: sources, transmission, speed in different media, echoes and reverberation, noise and music, characteristics of sound, vibration in strings, resonance, harmonics and overtones, air columns in open and closed pipes.

**Part 4: Fields**
1. Concept and properties of gravitational, electric and magnetic fields.
2. Gravitational field: acceleration due to gravity, Newton's law of gravitation, gravitational potential, escape velocity.
3. Electric field: electrostatics, Coulomb's law, field intensity, potential; capacitance.
4. Current electricity: cells, potential difference, circuits, conduction, energy and power, shunts and multipliers, resistivity and conductivity, measurement of current, p.d., resistance, e.m.f. and internal resistance.
5. Magnetic field: magnets and magnetic materials, magnetisation and demagnetisation, force on current-carrying conductors and moving charges, electromagnets, the earth's magnetic field.
6. Electromagnetic field: electromagnetic induction, Faraday's and Lenz's laws, motor and generator effect, inductance, eddy currents, power transmission and distribution.
7. Simple a.c. circuits: peak and r.m.s. values, series R-L-C circuits, reactance, impedance, vector diagrams, resonance, power.

**Part 5: Atomic and nuclear physics**
1. Structure of the atom: atomic models, energy quantisation, photoelectric effect, thermionic emission, X-rays.
2. Structure of the nucleus: composition, natural and artificial radioactivity, fusion and fission.
3. Wave-particle duality: electron diffraction, duality of matter.

**Recommended textbooks (per syllabus):** Ike, *Essential Principles of Physics* and *Numerical Problems and Solutions in Physics*; Nelson, *Fundamentals of Physics*; Nelson and Parker, *Advanced Level Physics*; Okeke and Anyakoha, *Senior Secondary School Physics*; Olumuyiwa and Ogunkoya, *Comprehensive Certificate Physics*.

## Agriculture / Agricultural Science (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-agric-syllabus/

Presented as an applied science. Schools offering it must run a school farm, keep at least one ruminant and one non-ruminant, and establish a fish pond where feasible. Candidates keep practical notebooks. A review of the JSS Agricultural Science syllabus is assumed.

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 50 minutes.
- Paper 2: six essay questions, answer five, 90 marks, 2 hours 10 minutes.
- Paper 3: practical, four questions, 60 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes.

**Topics**

1. **Basic concepts**
   - Meaning, branches and importance of agriculture.
   - Problems of agricultural development (land tenure, amenities, finance, transport, storage and processing, education and extension, tools, inputs, marketing, environmental degradation) and solutions.
   - Subsistence versus commercial agriculture.
   - Role of government (finance, education, extension, policy) and NGOs.
   - Agricultural laws and reforms: land tenure systems and the Land Use Act.
2. **Agricultural ecology**
   - Farm ecosystem; land and its uses; factors affecting land availability (physical, economic, socio-cultural).
   - Agro-allied industries and links between agriculture and industry.
   - Climatic, biotic and edaphic factors.
   - Rocks (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic); soil formation and profile; soil types, composition, pH, texture and structure.
   - Plant nutrients, deficiency symptoms, nutrient cycles (nitrogen, carbon, water, phosphorus); organic agriculture.
   - Irrigation; drainage; agricultural pollution.
3. **Agricultural engineering and mechanisation**
   - Simple farm tools; farm machinery and tractor-coupled implements; maintenance.
   - Mechanisation (advantages, limits, prospects); sources of farm power.
   - Farm surveying, farm planning, farmstead planning.
4. **Crop production**
   - Classification of crops (by use, life cycle, morphology).
   - Husbandry of representative crops: cereals, pulses, roots and tubers, vegetables, fruits, beverages, spices, oils, fibres, latex, sugar cane.
   - Pasture and forage crops; crop improvement and Mendel's laws.
5. **Forestry and ornamental plants**
   - Forest management, regulations, deforestation; agro-forestry (taungya, alley cropping, ley farming).
   - Ornamental plants: types, propagation, maintenance.
6. **Crop protection**
   - Crop diseases by crop group (causal organism, symptoms, control).
   - Insect and non-insect pests (classification by mouthparts, life cycles, control, side effects of control methods).
   - Weeds and weed control.
7. **Animal production**
   - Types and classification of farm animals; anatomy and physiology; reproduction and hormones; environmental physiology.
   - Livestock management; nutrition and rations; rangeland and pasture management.

## Further Mathematics (DONE)

Source: https://studentship.com.ng/waec-further-mathematics-syllabus/

The detailed contents and limits below come from the full WAEC syllabus text (2021/2022 edition, the latest full text I could open) at https://www.ecolebooks.com/waec-syllabus-for-further-mathematics-mathematics-elective-2021-2022-wassce/. That is a secondary copy, so check it against the syllabus your school holds. More challenging questions may also be set on General Mathematics topics.

**Aims:** further conceptual and manipulative skills; an intermediate course bridging elementary and higher mathematics; meeting the needs of future mathematicians, engineers and scientists; data analysis; logical and precise reasoning.

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 40 multiple-choice questions, 40 marks (30 Pure Mathematics, 4 Statistics and Probability, 6 Vectors and Mechanics).
- Paper 2: 100 marks. Section A has eight compulsory elementary questions (48 marks: 4 Pure, 2 Statistics and Probability, 2 Vectors and Mechanics). Section B has seven longer questions in three parts, and candidates answer four with at least one from each part (52 marks).
- Non-programmable, silent, cordless calculators allowed. Phones not allowed.

**Topics, contents and limits**

Questions may also be set on General Mathematics topics. Items marked (*) are tested in Section B of Paper 2 only; items marked (**) are peculiar to Nigeria.

**I. Pure Mathematics**
1. **Sets:** sets defined by a property and their notation; disjoint, universal and complement sets; Venn diagrams; commutative, associative and distributive laws; three-set problems and De Morgan's laws.
2. **Surds:** the four operations; rationalising denominators of forms such as a + b root n.
3. **Binary operations:** closure, commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identity elements and inverses.
4. **Logical reasoning:** validity of compound statements (implication, and, or, negation); truth tables.
5. **Functions:** domain, co-domain and range; one-to-one, onto, identity and constant mappings; inverse of a function; composite functions (simple algebraic functions).
6. **Polynomial functions:**
   - Linear functions, equations and inequalities: graphs, gradient and intercept forms, parallel and perpendicular lines, inequalities in two variables and linear programming.
   - Quadratic functions, equations and inequalities: graphs and turning points; completing the square; nature of roots (b2 - 4ac); sum and product of roots; quadratic inequalities; one linear and one quadratic simultaneous equation.
   - Cubic functions and equations: graphs; factorisation of cubics and of a3 plus or minus b3; remainder and factor theorems.
7. **Rational functions:** the four operations; zeros, domain and range; resolution into partial fractions (denominator factorising into linear and quadratic factors).
8. **Indices and logarithmic functions:** laws of indices and logarithms; solving index and log equations (including change of base); reducing y = ax^b to linear form; log graphs (*).
9. **Permutations and combinations:** arrangements and selections, using nPr and nCr.
10. **Binomial theorem:** expansion of (a + b)^n for positive integral n; approximation using (1 + x)^n for small x.
11. **Sequences and series:** finite and infinite sequences; arithmetic and geometric progressions; nth terms and sums; sum to infinity; recurrence series (*).
12. **Matrices and linear transformation:** order, types, equality; addition, subtraction and multiplication up to 3 x 3; determinants of 2 x 2 (3 x 3 (**)); inverse of a 2 x 2 matrix; solving simultaneous equations; images and matrices of linear transformations (reflections, rotation about the origin), inverse and composition of transformations; image of a line (*).
13. **Trigonometry:**
    - Ratios and rules: general angles 0 to 360 degrees; ratios of 30, 45 and 60 degrees; identities; radians and degrees; heights, distances, bearings, sine and cosine rules.
    - Compound and multiple angles: sin, cos and tan of (A plus or minus B); multiple angles up to 3A.
    - Functions and equations: graphs of sine, cosine and tangent and of a sin x + b cos x; solving trigonometric equations up to quadratic form; expressing a sin x + b cos x as R cos(x - alpha) or R sin(x + alpha) (*).
14. **Coordinate geometry:** midpoint, division of a line in a ratio, distance, gradient, equation of a line, angle between two lines, conditions for parallel and perpendicular lines, distance from a point to a line (*); loci; equation of a circle (centre-radius and general forms); tangents and normals to circles; the parabola y2 = 4ax including parametric form, with tangents and normals.
15. **Differentiation:** the idea of a limit; the derivative from first principles (simple cases); polynomials and trigonometric functions; product and quotient rules; implicit functions; transcendental functions such as e^ax, log and ln (**); second derivatives, rates of change and small changes; maxima and minima; tangents to curves; curve sketching to cubic functions; linear kinematics.
16. **Integration:** indefinite and definite integrals; integrals of polynomials, of x^-1 (giving ln x) (**), and simple trigonometric functions; integration by substitution (simple); plane areas and rates of change; volume of a solid of revolution; trapezium rule.

**II. Statistics and Probability**
1. **Statistics:** frequency and cumulative frequency tables; histograms (including unequal class intervals); ogive; mean, median, mode, quartiles and percentiles; mean of grouped data using an assumed mean; range, inter-quartile and semi inter-quartile range; mean deviation, variance and standard deviation; scatter diagrams and line of best fit; correlation; Spearman's rank coefficient (no ties); least squares regression of y on x (*).
2. **Probability:** meaning and relative frequency; sample spaces (for example two dice); equally likely, mutually exclusive, independent and conditional events; addition and multiplication of probabilities; binomial distribution; Poisson distribution (**).

**III. Vectors and Mechanics**
1. **Vectors:** scalar and vector quantities; representation in the form ai + bj; addition, subtraction and multiplication by scalars; triangle, parallelogram and polygon laws; unit and position vectors; resolution and composition of vectors, resultant of forces at a point; scalar (dot) product and its applications (angle between vectors, proof of trigonometric formulae); vector (cross) product (**).
2. **Statics:** definition and representation of a force; composition and resolution of coplanar forces (at a point and on rigid bodies); equilibrium of bodies and Lami's theorem; resultants; moments of forces; friction (smooth and rough planes, coefficient of friction).
3. **Dynamics:** displacement, velocity, acceleration and speed; rectilinear motion; Newton's laws (including inclined planes and motion under gravity); equations of motion v = u + at, s = ut + 1/2 at2, v2 = u2 + 2as; momentum and impulse, conservation of linear momentum; projectiles (**).

   - Pre-historic art (Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic); ancient Egyptian art (Old, Middle and New Kingdoms).

**Topics: Paper 3 (practical)**

13. **Distributive trade:** process of distribution; roles of producers, wholesalers, retailers, co-operatives; government agencies; problems and solutions.
14. **Population and labour market:** population size and growth, rural-urban migration, Malthusian theory, distribution, census, under-, optimum and over-population; labour force and human capital; labour supply and demand; wage determination; unemployment and underemployment; trade unions and employers' associations; government labour policy.
15. **Agriculture:** structure and systems (peasant, commercial, co-operative, state farming); importance; marketing and commodity boards; agricultural policy; problems and remedies.
16. **Industrialisation:** types of industry; plant, firm, industry, industrial estates; location and localisation; role in development; strategies and problems; link with agriculture.
17. **National income:** GDP, GNP, NNP and related concepts; measurement methods and problems; uses and limitations; trends and structure.
18. **Money and inflation:** definition, history (barter and its problems), types, characteristics, functions; supply and demand for money; value of money and price level; inflation (types, causes, effects, control).
19. **Financial institutions:** traditional institutions, Central Bank, commercial, development and merchant banks, insurance companies, building societies; money and capital markets.
20. **Public finance:** fiscal policy; sources of government revenue; taxation (types, objectives, merits, demerits, incidence, canons, rates); public expenditure (recurrent and capital); budget and national debt.
21. **Economic development and planning:** growth versus development; characteristics and problems of developing countries; development planning and types of plans.
22. **International trade and balance of payments:** domestic versus international trade; absolute and comparative advantage; terms of trade; commercial policy and tariffs; West African external trade; balance of payments, disequilibrium, adjustment and financing.
23. **Economic integration:** objectives, levels, features; ECOWAS.
24. **International economic organisations:** OPEC, ECA, IMF, IBRD, AfDB, UNCTAD and their relevance to West Africa.
25. **Major natural resources:** petroleum, gold, diamond, timber, groundnut; effects on West African economies.

## Commerce (DONE)

Source: WASSCE Commerce syllabus PDF (rachel.core2learn.org/modules/en-wassce/WASSCESYLABUS/COMMERCE.pdf).

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 50 marks, 50 minutes.
- Paper 2: eight essay questions, answer any five, 100 marks, 2 hours.

**Topics**

1. **Introduction:** definition, history, scope and functions of commerce and e-commerce.
2. **Occupation:** meaning; types (industrial, commercial, service); factors determining occupation; career opportunities.
3. **Production:** factors (land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship); primary, secondary, tertiary production; division of labour and specialisation (types, advantages, disadvantages, limits); link between production and exchange.
4. **Business units:** meaning and objectives of business; sole proprietorship, partnership, co-operative societies, credit unions and thrift societies, public enterprises, companies (formation, characteristics, comparison, advantages, disadvantages, sources of capital); amalgamations, mergers and acquisitions, trusts, holding companies and subsidiaries, consortium and cartel; dissolution and liquidation.
5. **Trade associations:** aims and functions; Chamber of Commerce, employers' associations; consumer associations and consumerism.
6. **Business capital and profits:** types of capital (authorised, called-up, paid-up, owned, circulating); credit (sources, instruments, functions); working capital calculation; profit (types, calculation); turnover.
7. **Trade**
   - Home trade: retail (functions, starting a retail business, success and failure factors, types of outlets from unit shops and stalls to supermarkets, chain stores, department stores, malls, hypermarkets, mail order; modern trends such as branding, self-service, vending machines, credit cards); wholesale (functions, types, elimination and survival of middlemen); channels of distribution.
   - Foreign trade: import, export, entrepot; terms of trade, balance of trade, balance of payments, bilateral and multilateral agreements, counter trade; tariffs and other barriers; functions of port and airport authorities, customs and excise, shipping, clearing and forwarding agents, export promotion council.
8. **Purchase and sale of goods:** documents (order, indent, consular and ordinary invoices, credit and debit notes, proforma invoice, letter of hypothecation, documentary credit, certificates of origin and inspection, insurance certificate); price quotations (trade, cash and quantity discounts, COD, CWO, CIF, FOB, E&OE, ex-works and others); terms and means of payment (legal tender, cheques, standing order, bank draft, bills of exchange, promissory note, transfers, traveller's cheques, e-payment).
9. **Finance and financial institutions**
   - Money: meaning, evolution, forms, qualities, functions.
   - Banks: central, commercial, development, mortgage, building societies, microfinance, bureau de change; banking services (ATM, transfers, e-payment); current, savings and fixed deposit accounts.
   - Insurance: principles (utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause); life and non-life types; insurable and uninsurable risks; importance; procedure; underwriting; re-insurance.
   - Capital market: raising funds (offer for sale, offer for subscription, rights issue, private placement, issue by tender); second-tier securities market; stock exchange (functions, transactions, speculators, types of securities); commodity exchange (tradable commodities, grading, standardising, trading methods, benefits).
10. **Transport, tourism, communication and warehousing:** forms of transport (land, water, air, pipeline) and documents (waybills, consignment notes, tickets, manifest); tourism; communication (oral, written, visual, post office, courier, telephone, satellite, internet and email); computer appreciation; warehousing (functions, types, advantages).
11. **Advertising:** roles, advantages, disadvantages; types (informative, persuasive, competitive, mass, specific); methods; media.
12. **Introduction to marketing:** marketing versus market, market research; marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion); customer services (pre- and after-sales); sales promotion (trade fairs, exhibitions, gifts, demonstrations); personal selling.
13. **Legal aspects of business:** contract (elements, discharge); agency (creation, duties, termination); Sale of Goods Act; Hire Purchase Act; employer and employee rights and obligations; patents, copyright, trade marks; business registration; consumer protection (consumerism, legislation such as food and drugs law, standards organisation, price control, factory, shops and offices law, product quality).
14. **Government policies relating to business:** commercialisation, privatisation, deregulation (meaning, reasons, advantages, disadvantages, comparison).
15. **Introduction to business management:** objectives; management functions; resources (man, money, materials, opportunities and goodwill); organisational structure (charts, departments, authority, delegation, responsibility, span of control); business environment (economic, political, competition, technological); social responsibility; inter- and intra-departmental communication.
16. **Economic groupings:** ECOWAS, Niger Basin Commission, Lake Chad Basin Commission, Mano River Union, European Union, West African Clearing House (history, membership, objectives, achievements, problems).

## Government (DONE)

Source: .../WASSCESYLABUS/GOVERNMENT.pdf. Sections B topics are country-specific; only the Nigerian items are listed.

**Aims:** define government and its principles, institutions and processes; understand the role of an informed citizen; evaluate successes, failures and problems of governments in West Africa; analyse constitutional developments in colonial and post-independence government; appreciate the country's role in the international community.

**Exam format**
- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions, 40 marks, 1 hour.
- Paper 2: essay, 60 marks, 2 hours. Section A (Elements of Government): five questions, answer two. Section B (Political and Constitutional Developments in West Africa and International Relations): five questions on the candidate's own country, answer two.

**Section A: Elements of government**
1. **Meaning and scope:** government as an institution of the state (definition of state, features, structure, functions); government as a process or art of governing; government as an academic field.
2. **Basic concepts and principles**
   - Concepts: state, society, nation, power, authority, legitimacy, sovereignty, democracy, political culture and socialisation, communalism, feudalism, oligarchy, liberalism, socialism, communism, capitalism, fascism, aristocracy, totalitarianism.
   - Principles: rule of law, fundamental human rights, separation of powers and checks and balances, constitutionalism, political participation, representative government, centralisation and decentralisation (delegated legislation, devolution).
3. **Constitutions:** definition, sources, functions, types and features (written and unwritten, rigid and flexible).
4. **Organs of government:** executive, legislature, judiciary; judicial independence; structure, membership, functions, powers, limitations.
5. **State structure and characteristics:** unitary, federal, confederal, presidential, parliamentary, monarchical, republican (meaning, features, merits, demerits).
6. **Citizenship:** meaning, acquisition, rights, duties and obligations, safeguarding citizens' rights.
7. **Political parties and party systems:** definition, structure, functions; types, merits, demerits.
8. **Pressure groups, public opinion and mass media:** types, operation, functions; formation and measurement of public opinion; roles and impact of mass media.
9. **Electoral systems and processes:** purpose of elections; franchise and its limitations; types of electoral systems with advantages and disadvantages; electoral management body (functions, problems).
10. **Public and civil service administration:** structure, characteristics and functions of the civil service; civil service commission; public corporations (purposes, control, commercialisation and privatisation); local government (structure, functions, revenue, control, problems).

**Section B: Political and constitutional developments in West Africa and international relations (Nigeria)**
11. **Pre-colonial political systems:** Hausa/Fulani, Yoruba and Igbo.
12. **Colonial administration:** British policy and structure (Crown Colony, Protectorate, Indirect Rule); French policy (assimilation and association, Loi Cadre); impact, advantages and disadvantages of colonial rule.
13. **Nationalism:** meaning, factors, effects; key nationalist leaders and movements.
14. **Constitutional developments**
    - Pre-independence: Clifford 1922, Richards 1946, Macpherson 1951, Lyttleton 1954.
    - Post-independence: Independence Constitution 1960, Republican Constitution 1963, the 1979 and 1989 Constitutions, the 1999 Constitution (origin, features, strengths and weaknesses).
15. **Major political parties:** formation, objectives, finance, achievements and failures.
16. **Military rule:** causes, effects and the various military regimes.
17. **Federal and unitary systems in West Africa:** origin, factors, structure, features, problems.
18. **Foreign policy:** definitions, factors, objectives, advantages and disadvantages.
19. **International organisations:** UNO, Commonwealth of Nations, African Union (including NEPAD), ECOWAS (origin, aims, achievements, problems).

**Reading list (selection):** Adigwe, *Essentials of Government*; Appadorai, *The Substance of Politics*; Echiefu, *Modern Government*; Price, *Political Institutions in West Africa*; Dare and Oyewole, *Government for Senior Secondary Schools*; Nofiu, *A-One in Government*.
